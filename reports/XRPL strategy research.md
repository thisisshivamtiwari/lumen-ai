# Strengthen XRP Detection With Trustworthy Context

The strongest defensible starting strategy is **a boosted tree ranking model built on correctly reconstructed payments, directed account behaviour, and bounded graph features, with validation that matches the permitted test context**. GitHub and Kaggle research strengthens the existing plans most through better payment semantics, harder legitimate comparisons, and protection against indirect label leakage. It does not establish a universally best model or a promised score. The biggest blind spot is treating every transfer leg, connected wallet, or static motif as the same kind of evidence: currency conversion creates execution paths, legitimate services connect unrelated customers, and suspicious wallets can also make legitimate payments. Preserve Strategy1's integrity and reproducibility discipline and Strategy2's domain features, but make advanced smoothing, ensembles, and GNNs earn their complexity through honest Average Precision (AP) improvements. This report proposes a revised experiment order and explicit decision gates; **no organiser dataset was available, no model was trained, and no performance gain has been measured**. The operational version is [Strategy3](../Strategies/Strategy3.md).

## Correct payment graphs before adding graph intelligence

**Build one immutable target table and two related graph views.** The target table contains every transaction requiring a prediction. The economic graph contains one sender-to-ultimate-receiver edge per reconstructed payment, retaining repeated parallel payments. The execution table retains asset changes, liquidity-provider legs, fees/spread evidence, and reconstruction quality. Count volume once at the relevant economic endpoint: source paid and destination delivered are separate measures, not amounts to sum across sequential conversion legs. A shared liquidity provider otherwise becomes a false laundering hub, creates giant components, and manufactures apparent cycles. The deck explicitly recommends one payment edge through the supplied helper, but its endpoint and ordering assumptions need reconciliation against raw records when released. ([Local deck, slides 8, 21 and 27](../Pdf/Decode%20%26%20Detect%20AML%20Training%20-%20final.pptx))

XRPL semantics explain why this matters. A single Payment can atomically exchange currencies through multiple paths, and `Account = Destination` can represent a normal currency conversion. `SendMax` is a spending ceiling rather than delivered value. For partial payments, the received amount is `delivered_amount`, not the requested amount; partial payment is a flag, not another transaction type. Failed ledger-included instructions can still incur a transaction cost and must not become completed money-flow edges. These are **defensive schema checks**, not claims that every real-ledger case occurs in this simplified challenge. ([Official Payment reference](https://xrpl.org/docs/references/protocol/transactions/types/payment); [partial payments](https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/payment-types/partial-payments); [payment monitoring](https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/payment-types/robustly-monitoring-for-payments))

Retain native amounts alongside USD and local normalisations. Parse XRP drops as integers and audit token decimal/scientific notation without losing exactness. Preserve the organiser's asset identity rather than merging equal symbols across issuers or blindly changing currency-code case. Join rates by the full documented asset/hour key, assert key uniqueness and row-count preservation, and keep missing/invalid-rate states explicit. Use the payment's containing UTC hour, not nearest hour or an outside price feed. Same-asset conservation and cross-asset USD compatibility measure different things: issuer transfer fees, conversion spread, and hourly price movement make a universal 10–15-drop residual tolerance unjustified. Use scale-aware absolute/relative errors as features, with known conversion uncertainty; do not deterministically label mismatches. ([Official currency formats](https://xrpl.org/docs/references/protocol/data-types/currency-formats); [transfer fees](https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/tokens/fungible-tokens/transfer-fees); [local deck, slide 22](../Pdf/Decode%20%26%20Detect%20AML%20Training%20-%20final.pptx))

Static motifs and chronological movement must remain distinguishable. A triangle can exist without a feasible A→B→C→A circulation; one atomic conversion's legs cannot be three independent laundering steps. Prefer bounded two-to-four-hop features with direction, lag, distinct transaction identifiers, asset compatibility, and amount-error summaries. If ledger index and `TransactionIndex` exist, use their documented ordering. Equal rounded timestamps alone do not establish causality; preserve ambiguity instead of ordering by parquet position or transaction hash. Keep static motif participation as a separate feature family: external AMLSim even implements an unordered cycle variant, illustrating why structural evidence and reconstructed flow should be tested separately, without assuming this organiser uses AMLSim. ([XRPL metadata](https://xrpl.org/docs/references/protocol/transactions/metadata); [ledger close times](https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/ledgers/ledger-close-times); [AMLSim cycle source](https://github.com/IBM/AMLSim/blob/master/src/main/java/amlsim/model/aml/CycleTypology.java))

The priority matrix translates the main blind spots into reviewable checks. P0 means correctness or leakage risk; P1 means high-value robustness work; P2 is conditional on schema and remaining time. These priorities are research recommendations, not measured feature importances.

| Priority | Blind spot | Improvement and acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | LP legs inflate volume, hop counts, cycles and degree | Reconcile economic endpoints against execution rows; preserve one prediction per transaction; never infer leg order from storage order. |
| P0 | Missing/failed flows disappear during joins | Left-join onto the full target spine; retain fallback features and status; create successful-flow edges only from supported evidence. |
| P0 | Repeated payments collapse into one graph edge | Preserve a directed multigraph and unique transaction keys; verify multiplicity after each transformation. |
| P0 | Busy service creates one giant component | Profile component sizes and positive-bearing groups before grouping folds; keep hubs and use bounded, role-specific evidence. |
| P0 | Neighbour prediction indirectly contains the held-out label | Evaluate supervised graph stage two under outer holdouts and inner cross-fitting, or omit it. |
| P0 | Rate/JSON errors become zeros or duplicate rows | Assert join uniqueness, coverage and parse-state counts; preserve unknown separately from valid zero. |
| P1 | Payroll, merchant collections, treasury and refunds resemble AML | Compare recurrence, counterparty diversity, outside connectivity, dispersion and local role; inspect false positives against these legitimate behaviours. |
| P1 | Mixed wallets inherit one permanent suspicious label | Keep edge-specific role and contrast features; test suspicious payments within otherwise legitimate hubs. |
| P1 | Split/gather or partial forwarding defeats exact matching | Add multi-window inflow/outflow compatibility and ambiguity counts; avoid asserting exact fund provenance or strict FIFO. |
| P1 | A week of observations masquerades as complete account history | Call wallets first-observed, not newly created; use observed net flow, not actual balance; audit cropped and boundary contexts. |
| P1 | One motif width/duration encodes a generator template | Use continuous widths, lags and dispersions across a few scales; test slower, wider, partial and overlapping motifs. |
| P1 | Tiny or very large transfers receive absolute exemptions | Keep amount×behaviour interactions and inspect both tails; rarity or size alone does not establish intent. |
| P1 | Tags fabricate customer identity | Scope tag statistics to their address; distinguish missing from zero; do not join actors across addresses by equal tag. |
| P2 | Search caps look like genuinely low motif counts | Emit truncation/cap indicators and inspect capped hubs; bound runtime explicitly. |
| P2 | Unsupported exotic transaction handling consumes the day | Profile first; add MPT/NFT/AMM/other semantics only when observed, relevant and documented. |

Tags warrant particular restraint: services control their meaning, and the same number at different addresses does not identify one customer. Similarly, an unseen account's existing balance is unknown; compatible onward payments are evidence of possible pass-through, not proof that a particular deposit funded an exit. Large amounts, rare assets, conversions, dust loops, and short delays can all be legitimate. The tiny-loop/pass-through anecdotes in deck slide 20 are public-ledger illustrations with unknown intent, not verified challenge negatives or safe whitelists. ([Official tags documentation](https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/transactions/source-and-destination-tags); [local deck, slides 7 and 20](../Pdf/Decode%20%26%20Detect%20AML%20Training%20-%20final.pptx))

## GitHub and Kaggle favour behaviour before complexity

IBM's author-maintained Multi-GNN implementation supplies useful engineering ideas: incoming/outgoing ports, interarrival times, edge updates, reverse message passing, ego identifiers and directed multigraph treatment. Its port numbers order distinct counterparties by earliest interaction; they are not simply transaction sequence numbers. Its theory concerns a particular combination of powerful directed architectures and adaptations, not a guarantee that a generic GCN learns every AML pattern. Its CUDA/PyTorch Geometric environment also makes adoption a different proposition from adding a few statistics to a tree model. ([Multi-GNN README](https://github.com/IBM/Multi-GNN/blob/main/README.md); [data utilities](https://github.com/IBM/Multi-GNN/blob/main/data_util.py); [author paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/29069/30025); [environment](https://github.com/IBM/Multi-GNN/blob/main/env.yml))

**The immediate feature order should be transaction fields → directed behaviour → bounded motif roles.** For sender and receiver separately, test incoming/outgoing counts, unique counterparties, volume, median/IQR, asset-conditioned variability, interarrival statistics, reciprocal activity, exposure, and new-counterparty fraction. Add pair recurrence and local amount/time contrasts. Preserve raw amounts plus log and robust relative representations, because absolute size and deviation from usual behaviour answer different questions. Port rank, normalised port rank and seen-before status are small ablations, with exposure controls. Motif features should describe this edge's participation as source, forwarding intermediary, collecting sink, or closing edge; proximity to a pattern is weaker than participating in it.

The Graph Feature Preprocessor research supports testing fan/vertex statistics and bounded patterns before replacing the classifier with a GNN. Its benchmark improvements concern minority-class F1, not this task's AP, and cannot be copied as expected gains. Prefer narrowly implemented documented ideas using already available packages. Snap ML's GFP API is stateful: `transform` inserts edges, while `fit` clears state, so an arbitrary whole-batch call does not prove causal behaviour. Current package metadata identifies IBM's ILAN terms, not Apache 2.0; listed macOS wheels are x86_64 rather than native arm64. On Apple Silicon, resolve terms and portability with a tiny smoke test before dependency commitment. An approved installed dependency and redistribution of one's own code are separate packaging questions; do not infer permission from another IBM repository's licence. ([GFP author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08593v2); [GFP API](https://snapml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/graph_preprocessor.html); [Snap ML package](https://pypi.org/project/snapml/); [installation](https://snapml.readthedocs.io/en/latest/installation.html))

Kaggle's most transferable lesson is entity-relative behaviour under honest validation. IEEE-CIS winner Chris Deotte's coauthored account describes client-derived behavioural aggregations, chronological checks, and multiple tree families while excluding the client identifier itself to reduce overfitting. The 21st-place author's own repository describes sequence-based reasoning and an offline/online discrepancy caused by constructing groups differently between training and inference. Those observations support aggregation consistency and cohort analysis, not importing card-specific features, external labels or an ROC-AUC winning score into XRPL AP. ([Winner's coauthored account](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/leveraging-machine-learning-to-detect-fraud-tips-to-developing-a-winning-kaggle-solution/); [21st-place author repository](https://github.com/white-bird/kaggle-ieee))

Adversarial validation should diagnose train/test differences, not automatically delete every separating feature. Absolute time can distinguish two expected windows; missingness can reveal a broken parser; asset mix can change without invalidating its target relationship. Kaggle accounts include both filtered and unfiltered branches, and a competitor's rank blend performed worse privately than its single LightGBM. These are counterexamples to guaranteed gains, not causal experiments proving that blending or filtering always fails. Compare paired supervised holdout predictions with and without suspect blocks and inspect semantic leakage. Do not copy leaderboard probing or pseudolabel tricks described in competition retrospectives. ([41st-place author writeup, indexed excerpt](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/writeups/lets-try-not-to-drop-1000-places-41st-place-soluti); [author retrospective, indexed excerpt](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/ieee-fraud-detection/discussion/111164))

This changes the proposed model budget. Start with a compact CatBoost or LightGBM baseline and a fixed modest configuration; model availability and a smoke test decide which runs first. A second family or alternate feature view is useful only if it corrects different errors. Compare the best single model, probability mean, and normalised rank mean on the same honest predictions. Rank averaging can reverse useful order and create equal rank sums; several seeds do not guarantee diversity. Keep full output precision and avoid random jitter as a supposed AP improvement. Defer a full GNN, broad hyperparameter sweep, generator installation and custom synthetic training-data generator until the sound baseline is complete. Small hand-reconciled fixtures are worthwhile for correctness, but without a matching performance oracle they cannot prove generalisation.

## Validation must protect every path from labels

The organiser's exact scorer should be the reference. Scikit-learn AP sums precision at score thresholds weighted by recall increments; trapezoidal PR-AUC and library metrics named `aucpr` or `PRAUC` are not automatically identical. Strictly increasing single-model remappings preserve ranking, whereas isotonic calibration can create ties. Consequently, remove threshold optimisation and default isotonic calibration from the AP path. Training by binary cross-entropy remains reasonable, but select features, checkpoints and blends by exact held-out AP. ([Official AP documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score.html); [calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html))

Choose the primary split only after release profiling. Prefer genuine scenario groups if supplied and representative; otherwise use wallet/pair overlap, timing, and documented generation mechanics to choose an appropriate holdout. **Connected components are diagnostics, not guaranteed independent scenarios.** A large legitimate hub can join most data into one component, destroying component CV. Random transaction splits can share wallets and scenario templates, inflating apparent generalisation to unseen scenarios. A single temporal split in seven days is noisy and potentially unrepresentative, but it remains a useful boundary/shift diagnostic; it is weak evidence, not meaningless evidence. Keep a small predefined matrix rather than searching for the split giving the highest score.

For every experiment, save paired predictions on the same rows, AP, positive/negative counts, fold and pooled-OOF AP, and top-ranked false positives/negatives. Pooled OOF AP includes ordering across fold score scales; mean-fold AP does not. Report them separately. Slice comparisons should include known/unseen endpoints, repeated/new pairs, hubs, conversion/self-conversion, tiny/large amounts, sparse/dense context, boundary/interior and parser/rate completeness. **AP depends on prevalence**: compare candidate models within the same slice, not raw AP across different-prevalence groups. An all-positive difficulty slice has degenerate AP; use a fixed justified negative background and recall among globally top-ranked transactions. Do not average slice AP into an invented competition metric.

The most dangerous optional feature is neighbour-model risk. Suppose edge A is held out for its own OOF score, but neighbour B's OOF prediction came from a model trained on A's label. Aggregating B's score into A indirectly leaks that held-out label. A single global OOF vector therefore does not validate graph stacking. For each **outer holdout**, exclude all outer-validation labels from every first-stage fit. Inside outer training, generate inner-cross-fitted scores for stage-two training; score validation/context using models trained only inside outer training, then evaluate the entire second stage on untouched outer labels. Exclude self/duplicate-leg contributions and match authorised topology visibility. Also check score-distribution differences between inner-fold training predictions and full-training inference predictions. Ordinary stacking documentation supports cross-validated base predictions, but the graph-specific contamination requires this stronger construction. If this cannot be audited before freeze, omit stage two. ([Official stacking documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.StackingClassifier.html))

A feature block passes only when it improves the fixed primary check, avoids a material unexplained hub/worst-fold regression, and fits the reproducible runtime budget. Tiny gains driven by one scenario deserve an optional paired scenario/entity uncertainty check, not a universal invented AP threshold. Shift diagnostics and cropped-window tests help explain failures; they do not replace labelled evaluation. Reserve an untouched confirmation holdout where positive/group counts permit it. Limit leaderboard use to checking submissions and confirming robust choices: adaptive score reuse can overfit, although research across Kaggle competitions does not establish inevitable substantial leaderboard overfitting. ([Dwork and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629); [Roelofs and colleagues](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/ee39e503b6bedf0c98c388b7e8589aca-Abstract.html))

## The revised plan prioritises verified gains and a complete submission

**Now:** retain the transaction spine, helper reconciliation, exact AP, behavioural baseline, and cheap directed graph summaries as mandatory. **Conditional:** add bounded static/chronological motifs, a complementary model, carefully gated blending, and properly nested stage two only after evidence supports each step. **Defer:** broad GNN adoption, automatic component smoothing, hardcoded generator templates, custom synthetic augmentation, large dependency migrations and leaderboard-driven pseudolabels. This is the strongest feasible starting strategy from the evidence, not a claim that trees must beat all GNNs on unseen data.

Use the actual available implementation time rather than assume a twelve-hour day. For a practical six-hour budget, spend 45 minutes auditing and producing a valid baseline, 75 minutes on behavioural features, 90 minutes on bounded motifs and hard-negative checks, 60 minutes on confirmation/optional blending, 30 minutes freezing and retraining, and **the final 60 minutes exclusively on submission and packaging**. With less time, remove optional complexity while preserving the baseline and last hour. Freeze the method by 15:30 London for the stated 16:30 deadline. Preserve exact IDs and documented order, finite bounded continuous scores, required columns, hashes, seed/configuration, dependency versions, visibility mode, and a rerunnable path to all predictions. Treat submission loading and the code ZIP size as completion checks, not clerical work after modelling. ([Local deck, slides 26–27](../Pdf/Decode%20%26%20Detect%20AML%20Training%20-%20final.pptx))

## Conclusion

The research changes the central question from how many suspicious shapes can be recognised to whether each transaction's evidence survives legitimate lookalikes, atomic conversion semantics, incomplete observation and an honest holdout. That distinction creates a useful stopping rule: an elaborate detector that cannot explain its hub behaviour, visibility assumptions or neighbour-label paths is not ready to replace a simpler verified ranker. The next decisive evidence must come from the organiser release and paired AP experiments; research has narrowed the experiment queue, not supplied an unearned winning score.
